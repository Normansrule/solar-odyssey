State of the field

Records, prices and capacity change every few months. Every number below carries the date it describes, the source that reported it and the day someone last checked it.

Most of this course is settled history and physics that will not change. These figures are different: they are snapshots of a moving field, so we keep them here, in one place, generated from the site's signed datasets. The other pages link here whenever they quote one.

How to read the dates

  • As of is the date the number describes: when a record was certified, or the year a price or a capacity total covers. Statistics lag: global price, capacity and cost figures are published months after the year they describe.
  • Last verified is the day someone on this project checked the number against its source. A gap between the two is normal; a last-verified date that is a year old means the page needs attention.
  • Record efficiencies are for small research cells measured by an independent lab, not panels you can buy. The page on efficiency over time explains the difference.
  • Active research Some records first appear as company announcements (with a test lab's certificate) before they reach the peer-reviewed efficiency tables. We mark these and update them when the tables catch up.

How to update a number

  1. Find the primary source: the certifying lab's report or the institution's announcement for a record; the agency dataset (IRENA, IEA, Ember, Our World in Data) for prices, capacity and costs. Check it says what you will write.
  2. Edit the data in data-src/, never in site/data/:
    • a new record: add a point to data-src/datasets/efficiency-records.json, and update its row in data-src/datasets/state-of-the-field.json;
    • a figure read from another dataset (price, capacity, share, cost): refresh that dataset (python3 scripts/fetch_raw.py for the Our World in Data files, or edit solar-deployment.json) and change the row's as_of;
    • a new source: add it to data-src/sources/records.json with today's date in verified.
  3. Set last_verified on the row and in the dataset's meta to today's date.
  4. Rebuild and re-sign the data, then run the tests: 
    python3 scripts/build_data.py
node scripts/sign-manifest.mjs
node --test "tests/unit/*.test.mjs"
    Signing matters: every page refuses to show a dataset whose hash does not match the signed manifest.
  5. Search the pages for the old number (for example grep -rn "35.5" site) and update any prose that quotes it, with its verified date.

The table is generated by state-of-the-field.js from state-of-the-field.json. A row either carries its own value or a from pointer ({ dataset, pick }) to the latest value in another signed dataset: module-prices.json, solar-deployment.json or electricity-mix.json. Pointers keep the page in step with the charts elsewhere on the site; tests/unit/records.test.mjs checks every row has a source, a unit and dated fields. The record rows are cross-checked against efficiency-records.json on the page: if the dataset holds a higher record than the row, the page flags it.
