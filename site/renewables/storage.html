Energy storage

The Sun sets just as people get home and switch everything on. Storage is how a grid keeps the afternoon's sunshine for the evening, and a windy week for a calm one: pump water uphill, charge a battery, split water into hydrogen, or heat a tank of salt.

17.3 %

of the world's electricity came from wind and solar in 2025, 5,492 TWh: the weather-driven sources that storage helps fit to demand. 2026-09-30

Pumped storage passed 200 GW worldwide in 2025. More dated figures on State of the field.

Every store of electricity is described by two numbers, and confusing them is the most common mistake in talking about storage. Power, in megawatts (MW), is how fast it can charge or discharge: the width of the pipe. Energy, in megawatt-hours (MWh), is how much it holds: the size of the tank. Divide one by the other and you get its duration. The third number is the round-trip efficiency: how much of what you put in comes back out.

From a Swiss reservoir to the water battery decade

  1. 1909

    An early pumped store. The Engeweiher plant above Schaffhausen, Switzerland, pumps water uphill to use it again later, and still does. The idea is older: a Zurich scheme pumped water for later use from 1882.

  2. 1928

    America's first large store. The Rocky River plant in Connecticut is completed: the first large-scale pumped-storage plant in the United States.

  3. 1991

    Lithium-ion goes on sale. Sony sells the first commercial lithium-ion cells, built on work by Whittingham (1970s), Goodenough (1980) and Yoshino (1985).

  4. 2017

    A battery for a state. The Hornsdale Power Reserve in South Australia, 100 MW / 129 MWh, becomes the largest lithium-ion battery in the world.

  5. 2019

    A Nobel for the battery. Goodenough, Whittingham and Yoshino share the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing lithium-ion batteries.

  6. 2023

    Batteries take over new builds. Global battery storage more than doubles to about 56 GW; batteries make up 83 % of new utility-scale storage capacity, while pumped hydro still holds about three quarters of the total.

  7. 2025

    The water battery passes 200 GW. A record 11.7 GW of pumped storage is added, taking the world past 201 GW, with about 243 GW more under construction. 2026-09-30

Four ways to store electricity

Batteries

A lithium-ion battery stores energy chemically: charging pushes lithium ions into one electrode, discharging lets them flow back. Two chemistries dominate: LFP (lithium iron phosphate), widely used for stationary storage, and NMC (nickel manganese cobalt), common in electric cars. They answer within about a second and return around 85 % of the energy put in. A flow battery keeps its energy in two tanks of liquid; bigger tanks mean more hours, which suits longer storage.

Pumped hydro

Pumped-storage hydropower uses two reservoirs at different heights. Surplus power pumps water up; when power is needed, the water runs back down through the same machines working as turbines. About 80 % of the energy comes back. It needs the right hills and years to build, but it lasts for decades and holds far more energy than all the world's grid batteries: by the industry body's count, more than 90 % of the world's stored electricity. Current consensus 2026-09-30

Hydrogen

Electrolysis splits water into hydrogen and oxygen; the hydrogen is compressed and stored, then a fuel cell or turbine turns it back into electricity. Every step loses energy, so only about 31 % comes back in a US Department of Energy baseline. Its strength is size: a salt cavern of hydrogen can store energy for weeks or seasons, which no battery can do cheaply. Active research

Heat

Heat is the cheapest thing to store. Concentrated solar plants keep many hours of heat in tanks of molten salt; hot-water tanks and heat pumps let homes and district-heating networks use electricity when it is plentiful and keep the warmth for later.

The numbers of storage

Duration

How long can a store keep going flat out? Divide the energy it holds by the power it can deliver.

t = \frac{E}{P}
t
duration at full power, hours (h)
E
usable energy capacity, megawatt-hours (MWh)
P
power rating, megawatts (MW)
Hornsdale as built in 2017, 129 MWh behind 100 MW: 129 ÷ 100 = 1.29 hours. A short-duration battery like this is better at fast jobs, such as steadying frequency, than at carrying a whole evening.

Energy in a raised lake

Lifting water stores gravitational energy, exactly as lifting a book does. A cubic metre of water weighs a tonne, so a big reservoir high on a hill holds a lot.

E = \rho\,V\,g\,h
\rho
density of water, 1,000 kg/m³
V
volume of water moved, m³
g
gravitational acceleration, 9.81 m/s²
h
height between the two reservoirs (the head), m
An illustrative upper reservoir of one million cubic metres, 300 m above the lower one: 1,000 × 10⁶ × 9.81 × 300 = 2.94 × 10¹² J, about 817 MWh. Recovered at around 80 %, that is some 650 MWh, enough to run a 1,000 MW evening peak for about 40 minutes.

Round-trip efficiency: losses multiply

A storage chain is only as good as the product of its steps. Each conversion keeps a fraction of the energy; multiply the fractions to see what survives the whole trip.

\eta_{\text{rt}} = \eta_1 \times \eta_2 \times \dots \times \eta_n
\eta_{\text{rt}}
round-trip efficiency: energy out ÷ energy in (no units)
\eta_i
efficiency of each step (electrolysis, compression, fuel cell, inverter …)
With an electrolyser that keeps 73 % and a fuel cell that keeps 51 %, the two conversions alone leave 0.73 × 0.51 = 0.372. Add compression, storage and the rest of the plant, and the Department of Energy's baseline falls to about 31 %.
A tonne of hydrogen holds 1,000 × 120 MJ/kg = 1.2 × 10¹¹ J (33 MWh, lower heating value). At 31 % round trip, every 33 MWh of electricity put into the chain gives back only about 10 MWh.
The widget's model, by hand: four hours of 100 MW demand, solar of 200 MW for the first two hours and none after, a 100 MW / 200 MWh store at 81 % round trip. The store takes in the 200 MWh surplus and returns 162 MWh, so 38 MWh of the last two hours must come from elsewhere: (400 − 38) ÷ 400 = 0.905 of demand met by solar.

Strengths, limits and where storage fits

Strengths

  • Turns cheap midday solar and windy nights into power for the evening peak.
  • Batteries react in about a second, steadying frequency after a fault.
  • Can be built close to demand, easing congested power lines.

Limits

  • Every store loses energy; hydrogen loses most of it.
  • Batteries are economical for hours, not weeks; seasonal storage is still an open problem. Active research
  • Pumped hydro needs suitable terrain and long construction times.
